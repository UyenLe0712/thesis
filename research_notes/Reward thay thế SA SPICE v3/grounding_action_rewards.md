## Q1. Referring-expression generation (REG) with a listener/comprehension reward: formulas, results, failure modes

### Takeaway
Since 2016, REG work has added "can a listener resolve it?" signals to generation. This has taken three forms: MMI or contrastive training, a learned listener used as an RL reward, and listener reranking. The gains are real but modest, typically +1 to +5 points of human comprehension. Every paper that pushes the listener signal hard reports the same failure: outputs that the model listener accepts but humans cannot use. The ones that work keep a likelihood or metric anchor, use a small listener weight, or use the listener only for reranking.

### Cited Findings
- **Mao et al., "Generation and Comprehension of Unambiguous Object Descriptions"** (CVPR 2016, not re-verified; arXiv 1511.02283). The model is trained with MMI. MMI-SoftMax maximises p(S|R,I) against other regions in the image, and MMI-MM is a max-margin variant comparing the target with one distractor region. Generation is evaluated end-to-end by running the comprehension model on generated sentences (precision@1, IoU>0.5; Table 1, "GEN" columns), plus AMT human preference. The authors write that CIDEr/BLEU/METEOR "can be unreliable and do not account for semantic meaning." — [arXiv 1511.02283](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02283)
- **Yu, Tan, Bansal, Berg, "A Joint Speaker-Listener-Reinforcer Model for Referring Expressions"** (CVPR 2017, not re-verified; arXiv 1612.09542). The **reinforcer** is a *separately trained* discriminative reward model: an LSTM sentence encoder concatenated with the object's visual feature, fed through an MLP to a logistic score in [0,1] trained with cross-entropy on matched/unmatched pairs. It is **frozen** after training. Its score F(w_1:T) on a *sampled* expression is the REINFORCE reward, ∇J = E[F·∇log p] (Eq. 4). Total loss: L = L^s_1 + L^s_2 + L^l − λ_r·J, with λ_r = 1 (Eq. 5, Sec. 4). — [arXiv 1612.09542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09542)
- Yu et al., results. Comprehension by the speaker module (Table 1, RefCOCO val / testA / testB): speaker 69.95 / 68.59 / 72.84; +listener 71.20 / 69.98 / 73.66; +reinforcer 71.88 / 70.18 / 73.01; +listener+reinforcer 72.46 / 71.10 / 74.01; the MMI variants are higher, e.g. speaker+listener+MMI 79.22 / 77.78 / 79.90. **Human evaluation** (Turkers click the referred object given the generated expression; Table 4, RefCOCO testA / testB, RefCOCO+ testA / testB): speaker+MMI 68.82 / 75.50 / 53.57 / 47.88; +reinforcer+MMI 71.08 / 76.09 / 55.16 / 48.50; +listener+reinforcer+MMI 74.08 / 76.44 / 56.92 / 53.23; adding listener **rerank** 76.95 / 78.10 / 58.85 / 58.20. In the authors' words, "the reinforcer module seems not to be as effective as the speaker" for comprehension. The largest human-eval gain comes from *reranking*, not from the RL reward alone. — [arXiv 1612.09542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09542)
- **Luo & Shakhnarovich, "Comprehension-guided referring expressions"** (CVPR 2017, not re-verified; arXiv 1701.03439). They use a comprehension model trained on human expressions as "a differentiable proxy of human evaluation" in two ways. (i) **Training by proxy**: L = L_gen + λ·L_com, with variants for compound loss, modified scheduled sampling and SMIXEC. (ii) **Generate-and-rerank**: sample about 100 candidates and pick the one the comprehension model best resolves. The failure mode is stated explicitly: without a floor ε on ground-truth exposure, the generator "will be purely guided by the comprehension model" and finds "pathological optimas… the generated expressions would do 'well' on comprehension model, but no longer be intelligible to human" (Sec. 4.1). Human click accuracy (Table 4, RefCOCO testA / testB, RefCOCO+ testA / testB): MMI 53/61/39/35; SMIXEC (training by proxy) 62/68/46/25; **Rerank 66/75/43/47**. Training by proxy *lost* on RefCOCO+ testB (25 vs MMI 35). — [arXiv 1701.03439](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03439)
- **Luo, Price, Cohen, Shakhnarovich, "Discriminability objective for training descriptive captions"** (CVPR 2018, not re-verified; arXiv 1803.04376). This is the cleanest precedent for **metric + listener, additive**: R(ĉ,I) = CIDEr(ĉ) − λ·L_CON(ĉ,I) (Eq. 11). L_CON is the contrastive loss of a *frozen, pre-trained* image–caption retrieval model, used with an SCST greedy baseline. The authors note that the listener-only reward "does not yield human-friendly captions" and that log-likelihood + λ·DISC was hard to train ("for small λ … stuck… increasing λ would abruptly make output less fluent"). The discriminability term "cooperates better with CIDEr." Table 2 (val) compares ATTN+CIDER with ATTN+CIDER+DISC(λ=1): retrieval accuracy 71.05 → 75.74; BLEU-4 .3592 → .3627; METEOR .2695 → .2728; ROUGE-L .5678 → .5706; CIDEr 1.1332 → 1.1406; SPICE .2083 → .2113. At λ=10, accuracy rises to 83.69 but CIDEr falls to 1.0552 and SPICE to .2070. **λ=1 was the best trade-off**, improving both metrics and discriminability. — [arXiv 1803.04376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04376)
- **Tanaka et al., "Generating Easy-to-Understand Referring Expressions for Target Identifications"** (ICCV 2019, not re-verified; arXiv 1811.12104). The speaker gets a Yu-style reinforcer reward via policy gradient, plus a ranking loss built from **human comprehension time and accuracy** annotations. The target is expressions "comprehended correctly and quickly by humans." — [arXiv 1811.12104](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12104)
- **Language drift under listener/task rewards.** Lazaridou, Potapenko, Tieleman, "Multi-agent Communication meets Natural Language" (ACL 2020, not re-verified; arXiv 2005.07064). Reward: +1/−1 for the listener picking the target image. They name three drift types: **structural** (ungrammatical; measured by log p(m) under an LM), **semantic** (measured by log p(m|i) under an image-conditioned captioner), and **pragmatic** (agent-listener success minus human-listener success). Joint-listener success "is not predictive of the human's one". The emergent-communication speaker is near-perfect with its joint listener but humans are at chance. Pure "reward finetuning" had the highest structural drift. Reward-learned **rerankers** (PoE, noisy channel) gave the best human success (Table 2: e.g. PoE 0.95/0.88/0.85/0.93 with GT captions). A **fixed** pre-trained grounded listener was "significantly predictive of the human performance (p < 0.005)". — [arXiv 2005.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07064)
- Lee, Cho, Kiela, "Countering Language Drift via Visual Grounding" (EMNLP 2019, not re-verified; arXiv 1909.04499). With a scalar task reward, "the communication protocol may easily and radically diverge from natural language". The best mitigation combines a **syntactic constraint (LM likelihood)** with a **semantic constraint (visual grounding)**. — [arXiv 1909.04499](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04499)

### Inferences
- In this line of work the listener is (a) frozen, (b) separately trained, and (c) mixed with a likelihood or metric term at a small weight. The project's "SPICE + small independent-grounder term" design matches the Luo et al. 2018 recipe. The precedent predicts **small metric gains (+0.3 SPICE-points scale) together with a usability gain**, not a large jump.
- Reranking by the listener (Yu Table 4; Luo & Shakhnarovich Table 4; Lazaridou Table 2) consistently beat RL-with-listener-reward on human comprehension. For this project, best-of-G reranking at inference with a non-UGround grounder is a precedent-backed alternative to putting the grounder in the reward.
- The "pathological optima" warning implies that any grounder reward in GRPO needs the KL anchor (β=0.04 already) plus the SPICE term. A pure grounder reward is not safe.

### Gaps
- The CVPR/ICCV/ACL/EMNLP venue strings for Mao, Yu, Luo & Shakhnarovich, Luo 2018, Tanaka, Lazaridou and Lee were not re-opened at CVF/ACL Anthology this session. They are well known but should be marked "not re-verified".
- I did not extract Tanaka et al.'s reward weights or result numbers.
- I found no REG paper using a modern VLM grounder as a GRPO reward on GUIs.

## Q2. Navigation / in-situ instruction generation with follower rewards

### Takeaway
Follower-based signals for instruction generation started as **pragmatic reranking** (Fried et al. 2018), not RL. The first GRPO-with-follower-reward paper I found (LaF-GRPO, 2025) reports only small gains from the follower term over format+METEOR rewards. A 2026 preprint explicitly lists "frozen-follower reward" as untried future work.

### Cited Findings
- **Fried et al., "Speaker-Follower Models for Vision-and-Language Navigation"** (NeurIPS 2018, verified in PDF header "32nd Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NIPS 2018)"). The speaker is used for data augmentation and for **pragmatic inference**. Candidate routes (K=40) are rescored by P_S(d|r)^λ·P_F(r|d)^(1−λ), with λ tuned on validation. The authors "found best performance with values of λ close to 1, relying mostly on the score of the speaker". No RL reward to the speaker. — [arXiv 1806.02724](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02724)
- **LaF-GRPO** ("In-Situ Navigation Instruction Generation for the Visually Impaired via GRPO with LLM-as-Follower Reward"; arXiv 2506.04070; an AAAI OJS download URL appeared in search, so venue from listing). The model is Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B with GRPO. A **text-only follower** (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, SFT'd to emit a structured action, >98% parse accuracy) reads the generated instruction. Reward r_LaF = 0.4·δ(direction) + 0.4·δ(distance) + 0.2·δ(alert), with exact-match indicators. It is combined with format and METEOR rewards, but the combination formula is not given explicitly. Ablation (Table 3, 3B, intra-town, no pre-calc), BLEU / ROUGE / METEOR / SPICE: with LaF 10.921 / .323 / .528 / .274 vs without LaF 10.912 / .317 / .525 / **.279**. The follower term moved text metrics by ≤0.006 and slightly *lowered* SPICE. SFT → SFT+LaF-GRPO: METEOR .496 → .528. — [arXiv 2506.04070](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04070v2); [AAAI OJS listing](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/40804/44765)
- **Spatially-Aware Speaker (SAS)**, ACL 2024 (ACL Anthology entry 2024.acl-long.734). Uses adversarial reward learning so as to "avoid systematic bias introduced by language evaluation metrics". Details not extracted. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.734/)
- **VTInstructor** (arXiv 2608.15284, preprint) says its policy is not optimized for navigation success and lists adding sparse frozen-follower feedback as future work (search summary only; not opened). — [arXiv 2608.15284](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15284)
- LANA (Wang et al.) jointly trains instruction following and generation and reports SPICE as the "primary metric" for generation (Sec. 4, read from PDF). I did not see an RL follower reward in the parts I grepped. — [arXiv 2303.08409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08409)

### Inferences
- LaF-GRPO is the closest structural analogue to this project: a 3B Qwen2.5-VL, GRPO, a text metric plus an "executability" reward from a separate model. Its near-null text-metric effect and its slight SPICE drop **match the project's own null result with the Phi-4 listener**. That supports reporting the listener reward as a "tried, not effective" ablation rather than expecting a gain.
- LaF-GRPO's follower reward is a **structured-action match**: direction/distance/alert, the analogue of action-type plus coarse location. That precedent supports an *action-type-match* term more than a full grounding term.

### Gaps
- LaF-GRPO does not report what the follower reward does to *follower/executability* accuracy, only text metrics. So whether it improves usability is unknown.
- I found no 2023–2025 VLN speaker paper trained with RL on a follower's navigation success. VTInstructor's statement suggests this gap is real, but it was not verified beyond the search summary.

## Q3. GUI-agent RL reward designs (format + action type + coordinate): components, weights, ablations

### Takeaway
R1-style GUI agents use **binary action-type match + binary point-in-box (or dense Gaussian) + format**, usually summed. Action type gets a low weight (0.2 in InfiGUI-R1) or equal weight (UI-R1). None of the papers checked publishes a clean "remove the action-type reward" ablation with numbers. Grounding-reward papers report that binary hit rewards invite hacking (box-size exploitation) and that dense Gaussian rewards beat binary hit rewards by about 6 points.

### Cited Findings
- **UI-R1** (arXiv 2503.21620; an AAAI OJS URL appeared in search, so venue from listing). R = R_T + R_C + R_F, an unweighted sum (Eq. 3). R_T = 1 if action type ∈ {Click, Scroll, Back, Open_App, Input_Text} matches; R_C = 1 if the click falls inside the GT box (Eq. 4). The paper says R_C beats an IoU>0.5 reward (Fig. 4, numbers only in the figure). It states that the action reward "does not always positively impact grounding tasks", attributed to ambiguity from a larger action space. There is no numeric action-reward ablation in the text. — [arXiv 2503.21620](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21620); [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/38816/42778)
- **GUI-R1** (arXiv 2504.10458v4, preprint). R_acc = R_act + R_point + R_text, with R_act and R_point binary and R_text = 1 if semantic F1 > 0.5. Total R_o = α·R_f + β·R_acc. Table 4: (α, β) = (0.2, 0.8) gives 49.75 vs 47.72 for (0.5, 0.5) and 46.48 for (0.8, 0.2). No per-component ablation. — [arXiv 2504.10458](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10458)
- **InfiGUI-R1-3B** (arXiv 2504.14239, preprint, "under review"; built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B). R_total = w_f·R_format + w_a·R_acc with **w_f = 0.1, w_a = 0.9**. **R_acc is computed only if R_format = 1, which is a gate.** Agent reward R_agent = w_t·R_type + w_p·R_param with **w_t = 0.2, w_p = 0.8**, where R_param = 1 only if both type and all parameters match. Grounding reward: point-in-box. — [arXiv 2504.14239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14239)
- **GUI-G1** (arXiv 2505.15810, preprint). "Reward functions based on hit signals or box area allow models to exploit box size, leading to reward hacking". Optimizing R_Hit alone shrinks boxes; R_IoU alone enlarges them (Fig. 3). Fixes: an added R_box size regulariser, removing GRPO length normalisation, and a difficulty weight. — [arXiv 2505.15810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15810)
- **GUI-G²** (arXiv 2507.15846; AAAI OJS article 40606 appeared in search, so venue from listing). Gaussian point reward R_point = exp(−½[(Δx/σx)² + (Δy/σy)²]) with σ = 0.5·box size (adaptive). Coverage reward = Bhattacharyya coefficient. R = 1.0·R_point + 1.0·R_coverage. ScreenSpot-v2 (Table 2): binary point 87.4, IoU 85.8, point+IoU 86.5, **GUI-G² 93.3**. Fixed σ 87.8 vs adaptive 93.3. Adding a format reward: 93.2. No action-type reward. — [arXiv 2507.15846](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15846); [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40606)
- **SE-GUI** (arXiv 2505.12370, preprint) uses a dense point reward in GRPO and has an MLLM generate element descriptions that are then grounded (search summary only). — [arXiv 2505.12370](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12370v1)
- A 2026 preprint on faithful mobile agents reports that reward distributions differ by action type. Coordinate rewards are spread out, while discrete choices concentrate near 0/1, "lead[ing] to advantage collapse" (search summary only). — [arXiv 2605.01208](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01208)

### Inferences
- In GUI agents, the action-type reward is a **low-weight, binary term** and is usually *dominated* by the coordinate term. That is the same structure as SA-SPICE-v3's 0.2·ActionMatch. The agent literature therefore does not show that a 0.2-weight additive action term protects rare action types.
- The dense Gaussian reward (GUI-G²) is the best-documented grounding reward. If the project adds any independent-grounder term, use Gaussian distance to the gold element's centre with σ ∝ box size, not a binary hit. That gives GRPO within-group variance even when no sample hits.

### Gaps
- No GUI paper I checked gives a numeric ablation that removes R_type. UI-R1 reports it only in a figure.
- The agent literature predicts *actions*, not *sentences*. Transferring their weights to a sentence-generation reward is an analogy, not evidence.

## Q4. Preventing action-type collapse (back/scroll becoming tap) when optimizing text rewards

### Takeaway
I found **no paper that directly studies non-tap → tap collapse under text-metric RL** for instruction generation. Three adjacent mechanisms have precedent: (1) **gating/zeroing** the reward when action type is wrong (InfiGUI-style gate; BiGrounder's cycle gate); (2) **action-type-stratified data/batch sampling** to counter click dominance; (3) **anchors to the reference distribution** (likelihood/LM constraint, minimum ground-truth exposure). The drift literature (Lee 2019; Lazaridou 2020; Luo & Shakhnarovich 2017) shows that unanchored reward optimisation drifts toward whatever the reward model under-penalises.

### Cited Findings
- GUI datasets have "a severe class imbalance problem" where click/tap is the vast majority. A proposed fix samples training batches **stratified by action type** ("Action Type Proportional Optimization"). Qwen-UI-Agent reports clicks/drag/type = 80.1% of primitive actions and raised long-tail actions to about 40% in its Action-RL data (search summary only; sources not opened). — [Qwen-UI-Agent report, arXiv 2607.28227](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28227); [AndroidControl-Curated, arXiv 2510.18488](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18488)
- One reported design assigns per-action-type adaptive weights to the parameter reward, and "a wrong action type zeroes out the reward", acting as a gate (search summary only; the specific paper was not identified with certainty). — [search summary listing arXiv 2512.02423 / 2609.36939](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02423)
- InfiGUI-R1 computes the accuracy reward only when format = 1, and R_param = 1 only when type AND parameters match. This is a hierarchical gate. — [arXiv 2504.14239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14239)
- Luo & Shakhnarovich keep a **minimum probability ε of ground-truth tokens** (modified scheduled sampling) so the generator does not become "purely guided by the comprehension model". — [arXiv 1701.03439](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03439)
- Lee et al.: an LM-likelihood plus visual-grounding constraint best preserves natural language under a scalar task reward. — [arXiv 1909.04499](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04499)
- Lazaridou et al.: multi-task training (captioning loss alongside reward) learned better than pure reward fine-tuning. Pure reward fine-tuning had the highest structural drift. — [arXiv 2005.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07064)

### Inferences
- **Why the additive 0.2·ActionMatch with AM ∈ {0.5, 1} is weak (inference, not measured).** The maximum reward gap it creates is 0.2×0.5 = 0.1. On a back step where all 8 samples are tap sentences, every sample gets AM = 0.5, so GRPO's group-normalised advantage from this term is **exactly zero**. The term can only help when at least one sample in the group already has the right verb. A multiplicative gate has the same group-variance limitation. Collapse on steps where the policy *never* samples the right type therefore needs a non-reward fix: an SFT/CE anchor on non-tap steps, stratified sampling, or a reference sample injected into the group.
- Text rewards favour tap sentences on back/scroll steps plausibly because SPICE gives partial credit to object tuples ("Settings icon"), which back/scroll gold sentences lack. A tap-style sentence that names screen objects can then out-score a terse "Go back". This is a hypothesis to check on the project's own `*_raw.jsonl` by comparing SPICE of "Go back" vs tap sentences against back gold. I have no published source for it.
- For non-tap steps, a cheap **exact-template reward** (e.g., 1 if a back/scroll verb class matches gold) acting **multiplicatively on SPICE**, or *replacing* SPICE on those steps, follows the InfiGUI hierarchical-gate pattern.

### Gaps
- No primary-source paper on click-dominance reweighting was opened. The ATPO and "zero on wrong type" claims come from search summaries and need verification before citation.
- No study measures text-metric RL turning non-tap instructions into tap instructions. This appears to be an open finding the project could report.

## Q5. Combining a metric reward with a verifier: gating (×) vs additive (+)

### Takeaway
The single direct ablation in a closely matching setting (BiGrounder, GUI instruction generation scored by BERTScore) shows that **gating the text reward on a grounding check beat the ungated text reward by +2.7 average points** (72.8 vs 70.1). REG precedent (Luo 2018) shows that **additive with a small weight** works when the listener is frozen and the metric is CIDEr. Recent multi-reward GRPO preprints mostly favour multiplicative/gated combination against hacking, but warn that hard gates on continuous rewards can over-suppress.

### Cited Findings
- **BiGrounder** (arXiv 2611.07765v1, preprint). The reverse direction generates instructions from coordinates (G=4, temperature 0.9, GRPO). Reward R_rev = BERTScore(x̂, x) **if b ≥ 0.85 AND c = 1, else 0** (Eq. 5). b is BERTScore-F (RoBERTa-large L17). c = 1 if at least one of K=4 forward grounding rollouts by the **same policy** reaches reward ≥ 0.9. Loss weights: reverse 0.2, forward 0.8. Table 5: Bi-SFT 67.6 → RL without cycle gate 70.1 → **RL with gate 72.8** (ScreenSpot-Pro 62.8 → 66.6; MMBench-GUI L2 83.5 → 87.5). Example (App. D): the distractor "Click the star to follow the repository." gets BERTScore 0.86 but fails the gate, so reward = 0. The authors argue BERTScore alone "rewards semantically fluent instructions even when they are visually incorrect". — [arXiv 2611.07765](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07765v1)
- **Luo et al. 2018**: additive CIDEr − λ·L_CON. λ=1 improved all metrics and discriminability; λ=10 traded about −0.08 CIDEr for +8.6 discriminability (Table 2). — [arXiv 1803.04376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04376)
- **InfiGUI-R1**: gate on format, then a weighted sum inside (0.2 type + 0.8 exact params). — [arXiv 2504.14239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14239)
- Multi-reward GRPO preprints (search summaries only; numbers not verified). (a) A multiplicative reward that gates a discriminator by correctness drives reward to zero once hacking is detected, whereas additive "can still receive positive reward despite producing hacked solutions" (large effect on countdown code, negligible on bug fixing). (b) A length-control paper found hard-threshold gated-additive variants underperform and produce overly short outputs, with multiplicative shaping acting as a soft gate. (c) MO-GRPO / per-dimension normalisation: joint GRPO normalisation can collapse distinct reward combinations into the same advantages, so normalise each reward channel before aggregating. (d) "Multi-Reward Hacking" reports additive GRPO collapse in several settings. — [MO-GRPO arXiv 2509.22047](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22047); [Multi-Reward Hacking arXiv 2610.21404](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21404); [Group Relative Reward Rescaling arXiv 2603.10535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.10535); [Right in the Right Way arXiv 2607.01181](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01181)

### Inferences
- For a binary verifier (action-type match, grounder hit), multiplicative gating and hard gating are equivalent. The open design choice is the **floor**: R = SPICE·(ε + (1−ε)·AM). Precedent supports ε = 0 (BiGrounder, InfiGUI). A small ε (e.g. 0.25) keeps a SPICE gradient between two wrong-type samples.
- SPICE is continuous and ActionMatch is binary, so per-channel normalisation (MO-GRPO style) before summing keeps the binary term from being swamped by SPICE variance. This is a cheap code change with no extra model calls.

### Gaps
- BiGrounder's gate uses the policy's *own* grounding, so it is a self-consistency check, not an independent grounder. Its numbers are grounding-benchmark gains, not instruction-quality gains, and it is an unreviewed preprint.
- The multi-reward hacking preprints were not opened. Their numbers should not be cited without reading them.

## Q6. A second, independent grounder (or cheap a11y/name check) as reward while evaluating with another

### Takeaway
There is precedent for a **separately trained, frozen listener** as reward (Yu 2017, Luo 2018, Lazaridou 2020) and for a **different-model verifier** in UI tasks (UI2Code^N uses GLM-4.5V to score renders). I found **no paper that trains GUI instruction generation with grounder A in the reward and evaluates with an independent grounder B**, and none that handles reward–evaluator *family overlap* explicitly. I also found **no paper using a string match on the gold element's accessibility name/text as a reward**.

### Cited Findings
- Yu 2017's reinforcer and Luo 2018's retrieval model are trained separately from the speaker and frozen. In Luo 2018 the evaluation also uses the same retrieval model ("Acc" column) plus human studies ("Acc-new"). — [arXiv 1612.09542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09542); [arXiv 1803.04376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04376)
- Lazaridou 2020: a **fixed** pre-trained grounded listener is significantly predictive of human listener success (p<0.005). A **jointly learned** listener is not predictive. — [arXiv 2005.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07064)
- UI2Code^N uses a separate multimodal model (GLM-4.5V) as a visual reward verifier, scoring 0–100 and normalising to [0,1] (search summary only). — [arXiv 2511.08195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08195)
- BiGrounder uses the same policy as verifier, not an independent grounder. — [arXiv 2611.07765](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07765v1)
- Targeted search for "element name string match reward" in GUI captioning/instruction RL returned no matching paper (search performed this session). — [search results incl. VISTA arXiv 2606.14579](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14579)

### Inferences
- Lazaridou's result is the argument for *why* an independent frozen grounder is acceptable as reward: fixed listeners track human success, co-adapted ones don't. The Goodhart risk with the evaluation grounder is exactly the co-adaptation case. A **different-family grounder in the reward, UGround only at evaluation** is defensible, but reviewers will still ask about family overlap. Most open GUI grounders are Qwen-based, so a non-Qwen reward grounder (or a non-neural a11y check) is safer.
- An **a11y-name mention reward** has no published precedent in GUI work. It is conceptually close to SPICE's object-tuple matching and to the REG goal of uniquely naming the target, so it would be a modest *methodological novelty*. The obvious risk is name stuffing, which needs a length penalty plus restricting the term to tap steps whose gold element has a clean name.

### Gaps
- No precedent found for reward-grounder ≠ eval-grounder in GUI instruction generation, or for quantifying reward–evaluator family overlap. The report should state this absence rather than imply a precedent.
- Compute cost of an extra 2–4B grounder per rollout on a T4 was not found in literature. It must be measured, not assumed.

## Q7. Implications: ranked reward terms to combine with SPICE in GRPO for this task

### Takeaway
Ranked by expected effect on the known harm (non-tap → tap) and on exec, relative to T4 cost and Goodhart risk: (1) a **multiplicative action-type gate** on SPICE plus **per-channel normalisation**, combined with a non-reward fix for groups that never sample the right type; (2) **action-type-stratified prompt sampling / CE anchor on non-tap steps**; (3) a **cheap a11y-name/OCR mention term** on tap steps only, capped and length-penalised; (4) a **dense Gaussian hit from a non-UGround, ideally non-Qwen grounder**, gated to tap steps, at small weight, or used only for best-of-G reranking. Distractor-contrast and MC-listener terms are deprioritised because the project already measured no gain and LaF-GRPO's analogous follower term was also near-null.

### Cited Findings
- **Rank 1: gate, R = SPICE × (ε + (1−ε)·AM) with ε ∈ [0, 0.25], AM from the verb-class regex, plus per-reward normalisation.** Precedent: BiGrounder gate +2.7 over ungated ([arXiv 2611.07765](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07765v1)); InfiGUI hierarchical gate and 0.2/0.8 type/param split ([arXiv 2504.14239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14239)); multiplicative-vs-additive anti-hacking evidence and per-channel normalisation (preprints, unverified: [MO-GRPO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22047)). Cost: zero extra model calls. Risk: no help when all G samples share the wrong type (zero within-group variance), and a hard ε=0 may over-suppress SPICE learning on hard steps (length-control preprint, [arXiv 2603.10535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.10535)).
- **Rank 2: action-type-stratified prompt sampling in the 2,000-prompt RL set, plus a CE/SFT anchor term on gold non-tap sentences.** Precedent: ATPO / long-tail upsampling (search-level, [arXiv 2607.28227](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28227)); minimum ground-truth exposure ([arXiv 1701.03439](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03439)); multi-task beats pure reward fine-tuning ([arXiv 2005.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07064)); LM-likelihood constraint ([arXiv 1909.04499](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04499)). Cost: none at inference, small at training. Risk: it is not a "reward term", so it changes the method description. It directly addresses the zero-variance failure of Rank 1.
- **Rank 3: name-mention term N = token-F1(gold a11y/OCR name, sentence) on tap steps, weight ≈ 0.1–0.2, capped at 1, with the existing length penalty.** Precedent: none found in GUI RL (Q6). Conceptual precedent: REG discriminativeness ([arXiv 1511.02283](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02283)). Cost: string ops only. Risk: name stuffing, and it duplicates SPICE's object tuples, so the marginal gain may be small. Project data show only 12.6% of a11y nodes are named, so OCR is the name source.
- **Rank 4: an independent grounder (not UGround; preferably not Qwen-based) giving a Gaussian distance reward (σ = 0.5·box) on tap steps, additive at λ ≈ 0.2–1 relative to normalised SPICE, or used only for best-of-G reranking at inference.** Precedent: frozen listener reward, λ=1 best in Luo 2018 (+4.7 discriminability, +0.003 SPICE; [arXiv 1803.04376](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04376)); Yu reinforcer, where reranking gave the largest human gain ([arXiv 1612.09542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.09542)); Gaussian beats binary by about 6 points for grounding training ([arXiv 2507.15846](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15846)). Cost: G=8 × 500 updates × batch grounder forward passes on T4, which must be measured. Risk: pathological optima / listener exploitation ([arXiv 1701.03439](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.03439)); a null result is likely, given the project's Phi-4 listener and LaF-GRPO's near-zero follower effect ([arXiv 2506.04070](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04070v2)).
- **Deprioritised:** distractor-contrast penalty and MC listener (already null in this project), and pure listener reward without a metric (drift: [arXiv 2005.07064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07064), [arXiv 1909.04499](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04499)).

### Inferences
- **Expected effect sizes (inference from precedents).** Ranks 1–2 target the documented harm (back 84 → 56/104 on dev; scroll −6.2 on test) and could recover most of the non-tap loss if groups contain at least one correct-type sample. On tap-step exec, every precedent points to **≤ +1 point**: Luo 2018 metric deltas are tiny, LaF-GRPO's are near-null, and the project's grounder-type rewards gave no gain. This is below the project's MDE of 2.11.
- **Sanity check before any GPU (0-GPU).** On existing GRPO+SPICE dev rollouts, if they are saved, count the share of back/scroll steps where at least one of the 8 samples has the correct verb class. If that share is low, Rank 1 alone cannot work and Rank 2 is required.
- To defend "SPICE kept as core" to the committee: Luo 2018 is the canonical precedent for *metric + small discriminability term* improving both the metric and usability, and BiGrounder is the GUI precedent for *gating a text reward by grounding success*.

### Gaps
- No direct published evidence that any of these terms fixes back/scroll collapse in instruction generation. The project would be first to report it, positive or negative.
- T4 throughput for an extra grounder in the GRPO loop is not documented anywhere I found.
- Several 2025–2026 claims (ATPO, multi-reward hacking, per-type weighting) come from search summaries only and should be opened before citing.
